Industrial perspectives

Industry 6.0: Building Resilient, Human-Centered Industry

A practical perspective on resilient operations, connected systems and the people who work with them.

From a label to a direction

Industry 6.0 is used to describe an evolving direction for industry rather than a single, universally fixed technical specification. At its center are systems that can adapt to changing conditions while keeping people, resilience and responsible use in view.

That perspective builds on connected production and automation, but asks a broader question: how can technology help industrial organizations respond to disruption without making operations harder to understand or control?

Design for resilience

Resilience is not one feature. It depends on visibility into operations, clear responsibilities, recoverable processes and the ability to keep essential work moving when a system or connection is unavailable. These properties need to be considered across people, processes and technology.

A useful starting point is to identify critical workflows, their dependencies and decisions that require human judgment. Teams can then test recovery paths and make trade-offs explicit instead of assuming connectivity alone creates resilience.

Keep people in the loop

Human-centered industry makes the state of a process and the reasons for a recommendation understandable to the people responsible for it. Automation can reduce repetitive work, while operators retain clear ways to review, intervene and escalate exceptions.

Industry 6.0 is most useful as a design prompt: build connected operations that remain understandable, adaptable and accountable as conditions change.
